I am Amber X. Chen, a PhD candidate in Social Psychology in the [Yu Emotion Science Lab (YES Lab)](https://yeslab.psych.ucsb.edu/) and Master's student in Computer Science at UCSB, expected to graduate in 2027.

I study how <span class="hl">person perception</span> is formed and updated in <span class="hl">social contexts</span>, and how people mentally represent their social world. My research focuses on how we integrate <span class="hl">visual information</span> (e.g., faces) and <span class="hl">conceptual knowledge</span> when forming impressions of others. Using behavioral trait ratings, <span class="hl">neuroimaging and eye-tracking</span>, combining with <span class="hl">visual and language models</span>, I examine these processes in controlled settings, such as when people view familiar figures like celebrities.

Extending this work to real-world settings, I investigate how person perception evolves within entire <span class="hl">social networks</span> over time. This approach allows me to uncover how the brain encodes <span class="hl">social structure</span> and builds <span class="hl">social cognitive maps</span> that guide our understanding of others and navigation in the social world. Ultimately, I aim to bridge person perception and social structure by revealing how individual impressions scale into representations of the broader social world.
